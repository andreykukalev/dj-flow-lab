# Musical language

- **Status:** working vocabulary, 2026-10-25. Written by Andrei (sections 1–4, kept as he gave them). Section 5 describes how the product uses it.
- **What it is:** these are not application features. They are a language for describing musical development and for thinking about transitions. Some concepts have been explored in depth; others are only established as useful working categories. It is not a definitive or universal DJ vocabulary.

There are two groups: core musical functions and track structure terms. They are related, but they are not the same thing.

## 1. Core musical functions

What the music is doing at a given moment.

| Term | Meaning | What the listener may feel |
|---|---|---|
| **OPEN** | Opens the musical context and introduces its language and elements. | Familiarity, space, possibility |
| **BUILD** | Creates anticipation, tension, or a sense of development. | Anticipation, movement toward something |
| **HOLD** | Sustains a musical state, groove, or tension that has already been established. | Stability, immersion, hypnosis |
| **RELEASE** | Releases tension or creates space, easing the pressure. | Relief, resolution, openness |
| **HANDOFF** | Transfers musical leadership from one track or element to another. | Shift of focus, natural continuation |
| **TRANSFORM** | Changes the character or perception of a musical state. | Surprise, a new interpretation |
| **RESOLVE** | Brings tension or a musical idea to resolution. | Completion, satisfaction |

Clarifications:

- BUILD does not necessarily mean increasing energy. Removing elements can also intensify anticipation.
- HOLD does not mean inactivity. The music can remain in a stable state while gradually changing its texture.
- RELEASE does not necessarily mean a drop or climax. Sometimes it is enough to remove the bass or create more space.
- HANDOFF does not always happen just once. During a long transition, leadership can shift between tracks several times.
- TRANSFORM and RESOLVE are useful working concepts, but their roles are not yet defined as extensively as those of OPEN, BUILD, HOLD and RELEASE.

## 2. Track structure terms

A type of section or structural moment, not a function. Their musical function depends on the context.

| Term | What it describes | Typical possible function |
|---|---|---|
| **INTRO** | The opening section of a track. | OPEN, introduction to the musical language |
| **OUTRO** | The closing section. | RELEASE, preparing space for the next track |
| **BREAKDOWN** | A section where the main elements change or are removed. | RELEASE, OPEN, or preparation for BUILD |
| **BUILD-UP** | A developing section preceding a significant musical event. | BUILD |
| **DROP** | A moment or section where significant musical energy returns or is dramatically revealed. | RELEASE, climax, or a new HOLD, depending on the context |
| **GROOVE** | Sustained rhythmic and physical movement in the music. | Often HOLD |
| **PHRASE** | A musically coherent unit of development. | Can serve any of these functions |
| **SECTION** | A larger block of the arrangement. | Can combine several functions |

One track's breakdown might create space for the OPEN of a second track, while another track's breakdown might create a BUILD toward its own drop. The section name alone does not determine its function.

## 3. How the concepts relate during musical development

One possible sequence of functions, not a universal rule:

OPEN (introduction) → BUILD (anticipation) → HOLD (immersion) → RELEASE (release of tension) → HANDOFF (transfer of leadership) → OPEN (new context)

This sequence helps describe a musical intention, but actual music can begin with HOLD, move into BUILD without increasing loudness, or shift leadership several times.

## 4. The principle that unifies the vocabulary

- Structure answers the question: **"Where are we in the track?"**
- Musical function answers: **"What is the music doing right now?"**
- For DJ mixing, a third question emerges: **"What role can this moment play in the relationship between two tracks?"**

This is what makes it possible to move beyond mechanically searching for eight or sixteen bars toward consciously choosing a musical moment.

## 5. How the product uses it

Each of the three questions has its own place, and they are never merged into one label.

| Question | In the product | Knowledge layer (brief §10) | Issues |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where are we in the track? | Structure terms on sections of the whole-track view: INTRO, GROOVE, BREAKDOWN, BUILD-UP, DROP, OUTRO. Phrases are the phrase grid. | _likely_ when the analysis proposes a section from its features; _your choice_ when the user sets or corrects it | #8, #29 |
| What is the music doing right now? | Functions on a section or a stretch of bars: OPEN, BUILD, HOLD, RELEASE, and TRANSFORM and RESOLVE when the user chooses them. | _interpretation_, always with the features it rests on ("BUILD: elements removed over 8 bars, low end gone"); never derived from the section name alone | #62, #63 |
| What role can this moment play between two tracks? | The outcome of a pairing, described as functions meeting: "A's RELEASE makes space for B's OPEN: a natural HANDOFF", or "B's BUILD starts while A is still in a HOLD: two tensions compete". Intentions on the Intend screen are written in the same words. | _interpretation_ | #56, #61, #63, #66 |

Rules for the product:

- **Structure is not function.** The app may suggest a function for a section, but only from what the music does there (energy trend, elements added or removed, low end present or not), shown with those reasons, and the user can change it. A BREAKDOWN label never implies RELEASE by itself.
- **HANDOFF can happen more than once.** The plan and the outcome allow several changes of leadership during one transition, for example mids first and bass later.
- **The less defined terms stay with the user.** TRANSFORM and RESOLVE appear when the user chooses them or when Andrei has reviewed an explanation that uses them; the analysis does not propose them yet.
- **The authored explanations and the AI explainer use this vocabulary**, in capitals as here, so the words stay recognisable from screen to screen. The S9 reference explanations (#64) are written in it.
- **Andrei owns the vocabulary.** Changes to sections 1–4 come from him; the product adapts to them.
