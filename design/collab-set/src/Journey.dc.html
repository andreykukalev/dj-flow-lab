Primary user

Two hobbyist or semi-regular DJs with a shared slot coming up: a B2B at a party, a bar or a small club. One usually drives the preparation; the other contributes between sessions.

Different libraries and tastes, often different experience. They own the music on their own computers and perform on a controller such as a DDJ-400 with rekordbox.
Job to be done

"When we have a set to play together, help us find the story in our music, agree on how it unfolds, and walk in on the night knowing what we will play and why."

The core problem

Not a lack of tracks. Two people have to converge on one intention and judge connections between music they do not both know, with no shared place to hear, compare and decide.

Four product models

A · Linear set planner

Good: everyone understands a playlist; close to how rekordbox works.
Risk: transitions become gaps between rows, alternatives vanish, and it drifts toward sorting by BPM and key, which existing tools already do.

B · Transition workspace

Good: deepest musical learning; it is the Transition Builder we already have.
Risk: no arc. A set becomes a chain of good pairs that may not tell any story.

C · Canvas of paths

Good: alternatives are visible; great for divergent thinking.
Risk: loses time, the one thing a set is made of; gets messy; hard to read as a play order; high effort for beginners.

D · Spine plus lab

recommended
Good: the arc and the moment, each in its own place. Concepts frame the spine; the lab is where a pair is judged by ear.
Trade-off: alternatives exist only per slot and per transition, never as a free graph. Less freedom than C, by design.

Assumptions to test first

The hardest part is judging pairings of unfamiliar tracks, not choosing tracks.Ask them to narrate their last prep; time each activity. Concept claims are trusted only if they can be heard.Count "Doesn't sound like that" presses and listen-backs per claim. Hearing two sections together beats auditioning full tracks.Time to a confident yes or no per pairing. Collaboration, simplest first

Prototype 1: one laptop, two DJs, every track, version, comment and pick tagged with who made it. Next: pass the set as a file between sessions. Plans, notes and decisions travel; audio stays on each computer and is matched by fingerprint. Later, if evidence asks for it: live co-editing in the browser.
